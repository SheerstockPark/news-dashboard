The Multi-Asset
Programme

The infrastructure for Sheerstock Park's expansion beyond oil — into indices, bonds and FX. Three phases: watch the new markets, understand what moves them, and prove the edge on paper before committing capital.

Why now

Two internal systems are already live and paying their way: the invoice-reconciliation dashboard and the News Terminal — a self-running market-intelligence platform (live dashboard, 06:00 & 20:00 AI briefings, a Saturday desk review, and urgent alerts within about a minute of major news). The Terminal was never just an oil tool: it was deliberately built as the first layer of a multi-asset operation — its scoring engine already ranks index and mega-cap moves alongside crude. This programme completes the picture.

Three principles

Decision-support only

Nothing here executes trades or gives buy/sell advice. The systems inform the humans; the humans trade. Zero regulatory exposure by design.

Zero running cost

Everything runs on the same free infrastructure as the Terminal — no subscriptions, no servers, no per-month fees. The only investment is build time.

Each phase stands alone

Every phase is independently useful from the day it ships. Stopping after any phase still leaves the firm better equipped than today.

Evidence before capital

The programme's end product is not a system — it's a defensible answer to "where is our edge, and how big should we trade it?"

The Multi-Asset Cockpit

The watching layer — the Terminal's proven machinery, pointed at every market

The Terminal already ingests 55+ sources every minute, scores every headline, and alerts on the big ones. Phase 1 extends that machinery to the expansion targets:

  • Rates & bonds — the US yield curve live (2y / 10y / 30y), bond futures, curve shape and how it's changing. The market that drives all the others.
  • Indices & volatility — S&P, Nasdaq, Dow, FTSE, DAX futures and the VIX, with the big-move detection that already flags mega-cap gaps.
  • FX & gold — the dollar regime, majors, and the haven complex.
  • Dedicated news coverage — rates, central-bank and index-market feeds added to the archive, scored with the same engine.
  • The daily regime read — one AI paragraph in every morning briefing: risk-on or risk-off, curve steepening or flattening, dollar direction, and what changed overnight. The desk starts every day oriented across all markets, not just oil.

Build: ~1 week of evenings · extends the live Terminal · data: existing free market feeds · running cost £0

02

The Event Reaction Engine

The understanding layer — because in rates and indices, the calendar is the market

Oil moves on surprises; indices and bonds move on scheduled moments — CPI, jobs numbers, Fed decisions, bond auctions. Fluency in these events is precisely what separates an experienced macro desk from a newcomer. This system compresses that learning curve:

  • The night before: a primer email — what's being released, what consensus expects, what's priced in, and how markets reacted the last six times this number surprised in either direction.
  • A reaction memory that compounds: automatically recorded for every release — "the S&P averages a ±1% move on CPI days; 10-year yields ±8bp; the reaction usually fades / extends by the close." The longer it runs, the smarter it gets.
  • Afterwards: the scorecard — consensus vs actual vs how each market actually moved, archived and searchable forever.
  • Integrated with the Terminal — event days are flagged in the briefings and on the dashboard's calendar bar.

Build: ~2 weeks of evenings · data: official US calendars + free price history · running cost £0

By the time real capital trades its first CPI print, the desk has already lived through dozens of them on paper — with the receipts.

The Paper Desk

The proving layer — the programme's flagship

A simulated trading book for the new markets. It answers the only question that matters before expansion: do we actually have an edge here — and in which market?

  • 30-second logging: instrument, direction, size, one-line thesis. Anyone on the desk can run a paper book — Neil, Josh, or both, tracked separately.
  • Real-price tracking: live P&L, win rate, average win vs average loss, drawdown — per person, per asset class, per thesis type.
  • Context captured at entry: the Terminal snapshots the headlines, sentiment and prices at the moment of every trade — so each one can later be reviewed against what was actually knowable, not hindsight.
  • The weekly review: an AI-written note every Saturday alongside the Desk Review — which theses worked, which markets suit the desk's style, what the book says about where the edge lives.
  • The quarterly evidence file: after ~13 weeks, a full report — the paper track record across indices, bonds and FX — on which the real-capital decision can be made with data rather than instinct. If the answer is "no edge yet," that's a cheap lesson; if it's "clear edge in X," it comes with the numbers to size it.

Build: ~2 weeks of evenings · no capital at risk, no execution, no regulatory exposure · running cost £0
