The Week the War Premium Died

Week ending Friday 4 July 2026 · covering 28 June – 4 July

⚡ Desk take: The market spent the week burying the Gulf war premium. A US–Iran deal to halt attacks, the Strait of Hormuz reopening, and OPEC+ shoving barrels back at the market took Brent to its biggest monthly decline since March 2020 — yet crude refused to break below the high-$60s, and gold's surge to fresh highs says the market doesn't fully believe the peace. The desk question for next week is whether the oversupply narrative or the fragile-truce narrative wins.

How the week unfolded

Tue 30 Jun Month-end tape: Brent books its biggest monthly decline since March 2020. Shipping traffic in the Strait of Hormuz visibly picks up; Oman positions itself in the widening talks.

Brent firms 2% into the close.

Theme 1 · From war to talks — but the peace is rentable, not owned

The de-escalation is real: attacks halted, talks progressing via Qatar and Oman, flows restarting. But almost every "peace" datapoint carries a hook. Iran is monetising the strait it no longer blocks — selling at a premium, offering China discounted Hormuz transit fees, floating tolls. Medvedev's "nuclear weapon" line and Iran's warning against "extra-regional" naval presence are reminders that reopening is a lever, not a gift. Khamenei's funeral week now adds succession politics to the mix. Desk read: the risk premium is suppressed, not extinguished — cheap crude calls and cheap VIX are the market handing out insurance at clearance prices.

Theme 2 · OPEC+ is pouring — and testing shale's pain threshold

June output up ~2.3M bpd, a fifth consecutive hike approved, Aramco cutting official prices into Asia: the group is reclaiming share into a softening market. Citi's $60 end-2026 call is the bear case going mainstream; the counterpoint is the "fiscal crisis" chatter around Riyadh — these hikes hurt the hiker too. Desk read: the barrel war caps rallies for now, but it is not costless for OPEC, and $60s crude with US stocks drawing is not a comfortable short either.

Theme 3 · The other war never stopped

While the Gulf de-escalated, Ukraine escalated — refinery fires, a Moscow fuel-hub strike, and by Friday drones reaching a St Petersburg oil terminal. Russian export infrastructure is squarely in the crosshairs at the exact moment the market has stopped pricing supply shocks. Desk read: this is the most under-priced bullish tail on the board.

Theme 4 · Cross-asset tells

  • Gold at 4,187, up 2.7% in a "peace" week — central-bank and haven demand is not buying the de-escalation story at face value.
  • Equities rotated rather than rallied blind: Dow strength vs a Nasdaq wobble, Tesla −7% on the week's last session, Apple +4.8% on product-cycle news, SpaceX now public and facing its first earnings.
  • Dollar drifting below 101 keeps a floor under commodities.
  • India expanding strategic reserves — a structural buyer stepping into every dip, alongside China's discounted-transit courtship of Tehran.
